## Phase 5 — Findings and fixes

### Fixed

**Q1-a — `SkeuWorkspacePill` painted itself with `Color.white`** (three call
sites: the chevron stroke, the pill's foreground, the dropdown rows). The
struct did not read `\.skeu` at all, which is why it reached for a literal.
This broke the design system's own acceptance test — "any screen must re-skin
correctly by changing only `.skeuTheme(...)`" — and CLAUDE.md lists
`hardcoded Color.white` on the reject-on-sight list. Now `skeu.ink` /
`skeu.inkMuted`.

**Q1-b — `Win95PinReplaceDialog` used `Color.black` for its scrim** (written
in this session). A scheme whose darkest tone is not black would have shown a
black veil over its own colours. Now `Win95.darkShadow`.

### Resolved by founder decision

**A1 — The light skeu palettes failed their own contrast requirement.**
Founder chose: light means light, text black (2026-08-16). Implemented — see
the "Light mode is light" commit. Cream now measures 10.9:1; the four derived
themes are lifted at their single base. Original analysis kept below.

`cream` is `ink: .white` on `canvas: #AA8360`. Measured: **3.42:1**.
`inkMuted: #F2E4D5` on the same canvas: **2.72:1**. `SkeuPalette.swift` itself
annotates `inkMuted` with "≥ 4.5:1 required", and the design system's
accessibility section repeats it. Primary text sits below the threshold for
secondary text.

This is NOT a bug in the sense of "someone made a mistake" — the whole look
was transcribed from a Figma frame that was dark, and the light variant kept
the same white-on-brown relationship at a lighter canvas. It reads as
intended, it just does not meet the contrast bar the project set for itself.

Three ways out, none of them a small edit:
1. Darken `ink` to a deep brown in the light palettes and keep the canvas.
   Correct by the numbers; changes the light theme's character completely.
2. Darken the canvas until white clears 4.5:1 (needs roughly #8A6A4C or
   darker). Keeps the white-on-brown look; makes "light" barely lighter
   than "dark".
3. Accept it and amend the design system to state the real target.
   Honest, but it is a real accessibility regression for anyone using it.

Affects all five themes' light variants, not just cream.

**Q7-a — the package stopped building for macOS**, and with it the whole test
suite. `PinnedTaskActivity` and `PinnedTaskIntents` guarded on
`canImport(ActivityKit)` / `canImport(AppIntents)`. Both modules ARE importable
on macOS — every type inside is marked unavailable instead — so the guard
compiled in and then failed on the protocol. `Package.swift` targets macOS for
the future Mac app, so `swift test` died before running a single test.
Narrowed to `#if os(iOS)`. **52 tests in 12 suites pass again.** Introduced
earlier the same day; caught here.

**Q4 — the depth ladder is bypassed, and there are two light sources.**

`SkeuDepth` defines six rungs with paired ambient+contact shadows. It reaches
the screen through `skeuSurface`, which has **three** call sites. The skeu
look is actually built from `skeuGlass` (15), `skeuTrough` (6) and
`SkeuCard` — each carrying its own hand-transcribed Figma shadow stack that
is on no rung: SkeuGlass casts three shadows, SkeuCard two at radius 68 and
124. Plus two screen-level sites (`SkeuMenu.swift:92`,
`SkeuSecondaryScreens.swift:134`) that hand-roll a pair inline. Every one is
tinted through `skeu.shadow` and scales with `shadowIntensity`, so Q3 still
holds — but "pick exactly one depth from the ladder" is not what the look
does, and the ladder is not the source of truth CLAUDE.md rule 7 says it is.

Sharper, and the reason this matters beyond bookkeeping: **the x offsets
disagree with each other.** The doc puts the light at 10 o'clock, vector
`(-0.25, -1.0)` — shadows should fall to the lower **right**, x positive.

| Source | x offset | Implied light |
|---|---|---|
| `SkeuDepth` ladder, `SkeuGlass` | `0` | directly above |
| `SkeuCard` | `-16.5`, `-63.9` | upper **right** |
| `SkeuMenu`, `SkeuSecondaryScreens` | `-4`/`-12`, `-6.5`/`-25.4` | upper **right** |

No site casts to the right. So nothing matches the documented vector, and
glass objects and card objects do not match each other — a pill lies under a
light from straight above while the card behind it is lit from 2 o'clock.
That is rule 6's "never invert this per component", and it is invisible until
the two sit edge to edge, which is exactly what the settings screen does.

Cheapest resolution is almost certainly to amend the doc to the light the
Figma frames were actually drawn under and zero the disagreement in
`SkeuGlass`/`SkeuDepth`, not to re-sign every transcribed shadow. Either way
it is a founder decision, since it changes the look.

#### New findings

**BUG — the add row keeps its draft after committing, and the title loses a
character.** Reproduced twice. When the text and the Return arrive in one
input batch, `commitAdd()` creates the task but `draft` is left holding the
typed text: the add row then renders as what looks like an ordinary task,
with an unchecked circle, **in every tab**, and there is no add row left to
type into. Typing `Rep1⏎` produced a task titled `Re1` — a character was
eaten as well.

Mechanism: `returnCommittingAdd` strips the newline, writes `draft`, and
calls `commitAdd()`, which clears `draft` and defers the store write. UIKit
then writes its own buffer back through the same binding, takes the
`guard new.contains("\n") else { draft = new; return }` branch, and restores
the draft the commit had just cleared.

Typing the text and the Return as two separate events does NOT reproduce it,
which is why this survived — but batched input is not exotic: paste, dictation,
autocorrect replacement and predictive-bar insertion all deliver it, and so
does a fast typist. `AddRowView` (Win95) is the same construction line for
line, so both looks carry it.

Not fixed here. The comments on both copies record two earlier rounds of
on-device debugging ("why intercepting only one of them fails on device"),
and a race fix I can only verify through synthetic input is exactly the kind
of change that regresses the path already paid for. The safe shape is
probably to pass the title into `commitAdd(title:)` instead of re-reading
`draft`, and to re-clear `draft` in the deferred `Task` — but that wants a
device check.

**§9 — "Windows 95" is a control label, not just the trademark line.** The
Design segment is labelled `Windows 95` in both looks. §9 bans the word in
any user-facing string. The About disclaimer is a defensible legal exception;
a segment label is a choice. Either §9 gets amended or the label becomes
something like `95` / `Classic`.

**The skeu secondary screens scroll under the status bar.** Settings and
Archive have no safe-area inset and no scrim: scrolled content passes behind
the clock and the Dynamic Island, and the "Settings" title and the Design
segment collide with them legibly. The Win95 window does not — its title bar
starts below the status bar. Two looks, two behaviours, and the founder sees
this on the first scroll of the settings screen.

**F11 — FR-011 was written for one look and never updated for the second.**
Both looks slide the list on a tab switch (`RootView:74`,
`SkeuRootView:223`), where FR-011 says tab switches have *no* transition.
The skeu look additionally fades and scales things into existence — the pin
and camera glyphs at `:474`/`:497`, the undo panel at `:637`, the workspace
dropdown at `:822` — where FR-011 says appearance is instant.

Those skeu transitions are **not** §9 violations: §9 governs the Win95 look,
and the skeu design system has its own motion rules. But FR-019 says every FR
holds in both looks, and FR-011 as written cannot hold in a soft-skeuomorphic
one. The honest reading is that FR-011 is a Win95-era requirement that the
second design language was never measured against. It needs splitting per
look, or narrowing to the Win95 look it describes. The round-4 §9 findings
stand regardless — those are in the look the ban actually governs.

**F17 — icon ships, launch screen does not match the spec.** `icon-1024.png`
